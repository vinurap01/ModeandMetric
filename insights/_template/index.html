Article title, written as a clear point of view

The introduction sits here: one or two sentences that tell the reader what the article argues and why it matters to their store.

Vin Perera 26 October 2026 6 min read

Body text is set at 18px with generous line spacing, in a column narrow enough to read comfortably. Paragraphs carry the argument; keep each one to a single idea. An article can be a few hundred words or around 1,500. The layout works at either length.

Use bold sparingly for the one phrase a skimming reader must not miss, and link to other articles or case studies where they genuinely help.

Use an h2 for each main section

Headings should read as a sequence: someone scanning only the h2s should still follow the argument. Never skip a level. An h3 always sits under an h2.

An h3 breaks a section into parts

Lists suit steps, options or a short set of related points:

  • Keep list items parallel in form.
  • Use a numbered list only when order matters.
  • Three to five items is usually enough.

A quotation or pull quote. Use it for a customer's own words or the single line worth remembering, not for decoration.

Name, role or source

  1. Wide figures extend past the text on larger screens.
  2. On phones they simply fill the column.
